How Many Sets Per Muscle Group to Build Muscle

By Ty Wamsher · June 16, 2026

If you've ever stared at a workout plan and wondered whether you're doing too little, too much, or somehow both at once, you're asking the right question. The number of sets per muscle group you perform each week — what coaches call training volume — is one of the biggest levers you have for building muscle. Get it right and you grow steadily. Get it wrong and you either spin your wheels or bury yourself under junk volume that just makes you sore and tired.

Let's clear up exactly how much you need, why the answer changes as you get more advanced, and how to count it without overthinking.

What Counts as a "Set" for Muscle Growth

When we talk about volume for hypertrophy, we mean hard working sets taken close to failure — usually within one to three reps of not being able to do another clean rep. A warm-up set with the empty bar doesn't count. A half-hearted set you stop at rep 8 with five more in the tank barely counts either.

The standard way to track this is sets per muscle group per week. So if you train chest twice a week and do 5 challenging sets each session, that's 10 weekly sets for chest. Simple.

The Numbers That Actually Matter

Across the research and decades of gym-floor experience, a few ranges hold up well for natural lifters:

Beginners: 8–12 sets per muscle group per week

If you've been training for less than a year, you don't need much to grow. Your body is highly responsive to the new stimulus, and your priority should be learning the lifts and building consistency — not chasing maximum volume. Eight to twelve hard sets per muscle group each week is plenty, and many beginners thrive on the lower end of that. Trying to do 20 sets per body part right out of the gate just means more soreness, worse recovery, and sloppier technique.

Intermediates: 12–18 sets per muscle group per week

After a year or two of consistent training, your easy gains slow down and you'll need to do more to keep progressing. Most intermediate lifters do well in the 12–18 set range. This is also where training frequency starts to matter: splitting that volume across two sessions per muscle group (say, 8 sets on Monday and 8 on Thursday) tends to produce better quality work than cramming all 16 sets into one brutal session.

Advanced lifters: 16–20+ sets per muscle group per week

Seasoned lifters who've squeezed out most of their newbie gains may need more volume to keep nudging the needle — sometimes 20 sets or more for stubborn body parts. But more isn't automatically better. Advanced lifters also have the recovery demands to match, and pushing volume too high without enough sleep and food just digs a hole. Volume is a tool you spend, not a trophy you collect.

Why More Isn't Always Better

There's a point for everyone where adding sets stops helping and starts hurting — researchers call it the point of diminishing returns, and beyond it, "junk volume." Your muscles grow during recovery, not during the workout itself. If you pile on so many sets that you can't recover between sessions, you blunt your progress, your strength on the big lifts stalls, and your joints start complaining.

A good rule of thumb: do the least amount of volume that still drives progress, and only add more when your gains genuinely stall. You can always add a set or two later. You can't un-ring the bell of an overuse injury.

How to Split Your Volume Across the Week

Hitting each muscle group two to three times per week generally beats blasting it once. Spreading 14 chest sets across two days means each session is fresher, your technique stays sharp, and you stimulate the muscle more often. This is exactly why training splits like Push/Pull/Legs (run twice a week) or Upper/Lower are so popular — they bake in that frequency automatically.

A once-a-week "bro split" can still build muscle, but you're leaving some efficiency on the table, especially as an intermediate.

How to Know If Your Volume Is Right

Forget the spreadsheet for a second and read the feedback your body gives you. Your volume is roughly dialed in when:

You're progressing. Over a span of weeks, you're adding reps or weight to your lifts. That's the clearest sign your volume is in the productive range.

You recover between sessions. You're a little sore, sure, but you walk into the next workout feeling capable — not wrecked, not dreading it.

You can complete your planned sets with good form. If your last few sets every session turn into a grind-fest of garbage reps, that's a sign you've got more volume than you can actually use.

If you've stalled for several weeks, are constantly beat up, or dread training, that's your cue to pull volume back — often the fix for a plateau is doing less, not more.

A Simple Starting Template

If you just want a number to run with, start here and adjust based on the feedback above: aim for around 10 hard sets per muscle group per week, split across two sessions, taking each set to within a couple reps of failure. Run it for 4–6 weeks. If you're progressing and recovering, hold steady or add a set or two to lagging areas. If you're stalling and run-down, trim back. That feedback loop will serve you far better than any rigid number on a page.

Bottom Line

Most natural lifters build muscle best on roughly 10–20 hard sets per muscle group per week, with beginners thriving on the lower end and advanced lifters needing more. Split that volume across two or three sessions, take your sets close to failure, and let your progress and recovery tell you when to add or cut. The right amount is the least that keeps you growing — not the most you can survive.
